## Summary (hand-written; the tables below are generated)

**No variant beats the market, and none makes money at DraftKings.**

- **Log loss:** all upgrades help a little. The best is +both (fatigue + serve/return on the tuned
  Elo): 0.5755, **0.0104 better than the current Elo** (0.5859) on the same 5,686 matches. It is
  still **0.0203 worse than Pinnacle's de-vigged price** (0.5552) and 0.018 worse than DK's (0.5572).
  Every variant is worse than Pinnacle in every season, 2020–2026.
- **Which upgrades matter:** surface blending (−0.0051) and tuning K/MOV/surface together (−0.0079)
  do most of the work. The serve/return model (−0.0094) and fatigue (−0.0092) add a little on top.
  Inactivity decay adds nothing (it is chosen as 0 in all but one season).
- **Betting at DK** on the model's edge ≥ x (x chosen walk-forward): every variant loses, from
  **−11.5% (+serve)** to −14.6% (base), all with 95% CIs below zero and CLV vs Pinnacle's close
  of −9% to −10.5%. The better log loss doesn't turn into bets that beat the close.
- **Verdict:** gate FAIL for every variant. Per the Lead's rule, the public page keeps the current
  Elo. +both is the best candidate if a model change is ever wanted, but it doesn't beat the market.

**Lookahead found and fixed during this study.** The first run of the fatigue feature counted a
player's later **QF** sets as "qualifying" sets (`round.startswith("Q")`), so players who went on to
reach the quarters looked more tired in their earlier matches. That leak made +fatigue and +both
look close to break-even (−1.2% and −0.5% ROI, 4,184 and 4,494 bets). With qualifying limited to
Q1–Q4 (regression test added), they lose −13.9% and −12.7%, like the rest. The tables below are the
corrected run.
