# The penultimate-set theory (1991–2026, results only)

## Answer
1. **Yes, but mostly because of straight sets.** Over all completed matches the penultimate-set winner wins ATP bo3 83.6% / ATP bo5 82.0% / WTA bo3 85.4%. In matches that go the distance (the only place the question has bite) the player who levelled wins the decider ATP bo3 53.0% / ATP bo5 54.5% / WTA bo3 53.2%: a small but real edge over the 50% that independent sets would give (SE ≈ 0.3–0.7 pp).
2. **Strength held equal, the momentum is ATP bo3 +2.8 pp / ATP bo5 +4.3 pp / WTA bo3 +3.0 pp** (symmetric test: the same favourite wins the decider more often when they were the one who levelled). It is steady across eras and levels.
3. **Favourite or underdog, the effect is about the same size.** A favourite who levelled wins ATP bo3 62.9% / ATP bo5 63.1% / WTA bo3 63.7%; an underdog who levelled wins ATP bo3 42.7% / ATP bo5 45.6% / WTA bo3 42.4%. So a levelling underdog is still the underdog in the decider: they win less than half the time.
4. **This is not a betting edge by itself.** Question 2 (does the levelling player win more often than the *odds* imply?) needs in-match prices: an in-play market at 1-1 sees the score and may already price momentum. That's the Kalshi test (`upsets/decider.py`).
5. **Caveats.** Retirements are excluded (~3% of matches; a player fading physically may retire in the decider, so momentum could be a little larger). The data can't tell psychology from in-match information (fitness, an injury, tactics, conditions): it only shows the latest set predicts the decider better than an earlier one.

Data: Jeff Sackmann's tennis_atp / tennis_wta match files (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), archival fork phat-d0/tennis-sackmann-archive. Generated by `python -m upsets.penultimate`.

Completed tour-level matches (no retirements, walkovers, defaults or unfinished matches). `rate` = how often the penultimate-set winner won the match; `expected` = their strength-only chance (all matches: pre-match Elo match probability; deciders: the per-set probability it implies); `gap` = rate − expected (± `gap_se`). Favourite = better pre-match Elo unless the table says ranking.
